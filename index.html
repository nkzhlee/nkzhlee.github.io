I develop human-centered AI for education, translating educational goals and expert knowledge into computational tasks. My work combines algorithms, benchmark datasets, and systems for assessment and feedback, instructional material evaluation, early literacy, and parent–child interaction. I collaborate with educators to build reliable, responsible AI that supports teaching, learning, and human connection.

Research Experience

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from The Pennsylvania State University in 2024, advised by Dr. Rebecca J. Passonneau in the Natural Language Processing Lab. My dissertation on automated assessment of STEM reasoning received the Best Doctoral Dissertation Award. Before joining UT San Antonio, I was a postdoctoral associate at the University at Buffalo’s Institute for Artificial Intelligence and Data Science (IAD) and Department of Computer Science and Engineering (CSE), mentored by Dr. Jinjun Xiong.

2026 · Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders

Parent’s AI Coach (PaiCoach): A Single-Case Experimental Study of an AI-Supported Parent Coaching System for Autistic Children

PaiCoach combines AI-generated feedback with human review to support parent-led communication interventions. A four-family single-case study found improvements in parent implementation and child responsiveness, while showing the continued need for expert oversight.

AIED 2026 · Companion proceedings, pp. 498–504

Design and Feasibility of an LLM-Powered Humanoid Robot as a Reading Companion to Support Children’s AI Literacy

An LLM-powered humanoid robot combines speech, vision, and gesture during shared reading about AI. Comparing three dialogue policies on real hardware identifies how structured interaction and bounded language-model support can improve pedagogical coherence.

PaperFull citation

AIED 2026, pp. 539–554

SciEval: A Benchmark for Automatic Evaluation of K–12 Science Instructional Materials

SciEval benchmarks how well language models evaluate K-12 science instructional materials against an educator-designed rubric. It contains 273 lessons annotated across 13 criteria, with scores and supporting rationales, and tests whether domain-specific fine-tuning improves evaluation.

PaperFull citation

LAK 2026 Companion Proceedings, pp. 246–248

LLM4Art: Using Large Language Models for Art Interpretation

LLM4Art uses expert-informed prompts and rubrics to examine visual analysis, historical context, and cultural significance. A 50-artwork benchmark compares five language models, and a web application makes the interpretation workflow available to users.

Computing Conference 2026 · Intelligent Computing, pp. 481–496

Judging the Judges: Human Validation of Multi-LLM Evaluation for High-Quality K–12 Science Instructional Materials

Two science education experts review evaluations from GPT, Claude, and Gemini across 12 curriculum units. The study distinguishes agreement on numerical scores from agreement on reasoning and suggestions, revealing where model judgments depart from educational expertise.

AIED 2025, pp. 285–292

StoryLab: Empowering Personalized Learning for Children Through Teacher-Guided Multimodal Story Generation

StoryLab helps K-2 teachers create personalized stories with illustrations, narration, and vocabulary support. Teachers specify learning goals and review generated materials, connecting children's interests with classroom literacy objectives.
